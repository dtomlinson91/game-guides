# Engage beginner guide

A reference for a first playthrough of *Fire Emblem Engage* on Nintendo Switch,
at final patch 2.0.0, on **Normal or Hard**. It is for a player who is new to
Fire Emblem and found the game's many systems overwhelming. It answers six
questions: when to grind, how class changes work, how to spend time on the
Somniel, how to build a team, how the rings work, and whether region donations
are worth the gold.

Read [Quick answers](#quick-answers) and
[Between every chapter](#between-every-chapter) first. Use the rest as a lookup
while you play.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **You cannot brick this save.** Nothing expires on a timer, and there is no
> calendar. Paralogues (side chapters) stay open until the end of the game.
> Master Seals and Second Seals become unlimited in the shop from Chapter 18.
> The side battles you can repeat always scale to your army's level. Only a few
> things are truly one-way:
>
> - The **Classic or Casual** choice you make when you start a new game.
> - A unit that dies in **Classic** mode.
> - Two recruits who join **only** if you talk to them: **Seadall** in Chapter
>   15 and **Lindon** in Chapter 18. See [Missable content](#missable-content).
> - Skills you forget to inherit **before Chapter 10**. See
>   [The Chapter 10 warning](#the-chapter-10-warning).

> [!TIP]
> Pick **Casual** mode. A unit who falls in battle comes back after the map.
> Pick **Normal** if you want the story first. It gives unlimited rewinds. You
> can lower the difficulty later at the bed in your room on the Somniel, but you
> can never raise it. Full detail is in
> [Difficulty and modes](#difficulty-and-modes).

## Quick answers

The short answer to each question you asked. Each row links to the full section.

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| **When should I grind?** | Almost never. Grind only when you lose a chapter again and again even with rewinds. Do the paralogues first, because they are free EXP that does not scale. [When to grind](#when-to-grind) |
| **How do I change class?** | Use a **Master Seal** to promote at level 10 or higher. Promote as soon as you can. The class the game "defaults" a unit to is the promotion its own weapons allow. See the roster table. [Changing classes](#changing-classes) |
| **How should I spend free time?** | Arena, meal, ring skills, dogs on the farm, then move on. Skip fishing, workouts and cosmetics. [Between every chapter](#between-every-chapter) |
| **How do I build a team?** | Train about 12 units. Bring 2 or more healers and 1 or 2 bow users. Bench early units freely when stronger ones join after Chapter 11. On Normal and Hard almost any team works. [Team building](#team-building) |
| **How do the rings work?** | Equip one Emblem ring per unit. Engage often, not just for bosses. The lasting value is the skills you **inherit** with SP. [Emblem rings](#emblem-rings) |
| **Can I pass rings around?** | Yes, freely, between battles. Bond level, inherited skills and weapon proficiencies stay with the unit when the ring moves on. [What stays, what goes](#what-stays-what-goes) |
| **Can I engage more than once?** | Yes. As many times per map as the meter allows. The meter starts full on every map. [Sync and engage](#sync-and-engage) |
| **Can I keep my early favourites?** | Yes. Early units have similar growth rates. Late joiners just arrive at a higher level. You can catch a unit up with no story cost. [Keeping early favourites](#keeping-early-favourites) |
| **Should I level up regions?** | A little. Level 2 in each country costs 5,000 gold and pays back in forging ore and food. Brodia is worth level 3. Levels 4 and 5 are a waste. Skipping them costs you nothing important. [Region donations](#region-donations) |

## Between every chapter

This is the routine you repeat after every battle. Do these steps in order,
then start the next chapter. The whole routine takes a few minutes once you know
it. Do not try to do everything on the Somniel every visit. Players who try
are the ones who burn out.

1. **Claim achievement rewards.** They give Bond Fragments, the Somniel
   currency. Players report the board at the top left of the café area.
2. **Check for new paralogues** on the world map. Do one when its recommended
   level is close to your team's level. See [When to grind](#when-to-grind).
3. **Arena: up to 3 fights.** Give each fighter a **Bond Ring**, not an Emblem
   ring. An Emblem ring earns no SP in the arena. A Bond Ring does. Fights
   against an ally also build support points. Nobody can die in the arena.
4. **Ring Chamber: inherit skills.** Spend SP on the skills in
   [Skill inheritance](#skill-inheritance). Watch any waiting bond
   conversations. Bond EXP stops at levels 5, 10 and 20 until you watch them.
   Polish a ring for a small bond gain.
5. **Café: eat one meal.** It buffs the **whole army** for the next battle.
   Invite two units to build their support.
6. **Farm: keep dogs.** The farm holds 5 animals. Dogs give iron, steel and
   silver ingots, which you need to upgrade weapons. Other animals give food,
   which matters little.
7. **Shops and smithy.** Buy staffs and replace broken weapons. Spend ingots to
   upgrade the weapons your main units use.
8. **Rearrange rings** if a new unit or Emblem joined.

> [!TIP]
> Skip these without guilt: fishing, Alear's push-ups, sit-ups and squats (the
> buff lasts one battle), petting Sommie, the Wyvern Ride, and all cosmetics.
> They give small amounts of Bond Fragments and nothing else. See
> [Somniel activities](#somniel-activities) for the full list.

## When to grind

This answers question 1. The short version: grinding is rarely needed, and it
often makes the game worse. Engage gives you a lot of free experience (EXP)
without it.

### Grind only when

Use this test before you grind. Grind only if at least one of these is true.

- You lost the same chapter several times, **and** rewinds did not fix it.
- You want to bring back a benched unit who is now far behind.
- You want to raise a unit to level 10 for a Master Seal.

If none of these is true, play the next story chapter.

### Free EXP first

These give EXP without grinding. Use them before any skirmish.

1. **Paralogues.** Base-game paralogues **do not scale**. Do one when its
   recommended level matches the next story chapter. If one is too hard, leave
   it and come back. Nothing is lost. Lyn's paralogue is widely called the
   hardest map, so wait on it.
2. **Arena.** Up to 3 fights between chapters. EXP and SP, with no risk.
3. **Staffs.** A healer earns EXP for every heal, whatever their stats.
   Micaiah's ring makes this stronger.
4. **EXP boosters.** Byleth's **Mentorship** skill gives 1.2 times EXP to the
   user and adjacent allies. It costs 250 SP to inherit. Marth's engage weapon
   **Mercurius** and Lucina's engage weapon **Parthia** double EXP. Both need
   bond level 10 and work only while engaged.

For bringing a benched favourite back up to strength, see
[Keeping early favourites](#keeping-early-favourites).

### How skirmishes work

Skirmishes are the random battles that appear on the world map after Chapter 6.
They cost no in-game time. But they are a poor tool for a weak unit.

- **They scale to your strongest units.** Enemy level equals the average level
  of your highest-level units, counting as many units as the map lets you
  deploy, plus 2. The boss is 5 levels higher still. A weak unit is **behind**
  in every skirmish.
- They appear over real time (one per 3 hours on Normal and Hard), and after
  you clear battles (one per 3 battles on Normal, one per 5 on Hard).
- Many players find them **harder than the story**.
- Enemies marked **Gold Corrupted** drop gold. **Silver Corrupted** enemies give
  extra EXP.

> [!TIP]
> **Training battles** are the safe grind. They are skirmishes that appear at
> Firene Castle, Brodia Castle or Solm Palace. In these, **nobody dies
> permanently, even in Classic**. Every survivor gets 30 EXP, and you get 1,000
> gold for each donation level you hold with that country.

### How long to grind

One or two skirmishes, then return to the story. If you still lose after that,
the problem is usually positioning, not levels. Read
[Combat basics](#combat-basics).

> [!WARNING]
> Too much grinding makes later chapters boring. Players who overlevel report
> that they could "mindlessly advance" and win. It also makes skirmishes harder
> for everyone else, because skirmishes scale to your top units.

## Keeping early favourites

Yes, you can keep your early characters all game, and you can catch a benched
one back up **without advancing the story**. Grinding in Engage costs only
real time. This section explains why early units fall behind, and the most
efficient way to bring one back.

### Why they fall behind

Early units do **not** have worse growth rates. They fall behind because late
joiners arrive already promoted, with many free levels.

- Serenes Forest's growth tables give early joiners a combined personal growth
  of about 266 on average, and late joiners about 284. That is a gap of about 2
  percentage points per stat. Chloé (310) and Céline (305) grow better than
  most late joiners.
- Kagetsu, Panette, Merrin and Pandreo all join as **level 1 in an advanced
  class**, with stats to match. One player called the Solm recruits of Chapters 12
  and 13 "15 FREE levels".
- Level-matched comparisons in the same player essay show the gap nearly closes.
  Chloé against Pandreo and Amber against Panette come out close once both
  reach the same total level.
- A unit at level 20 in an advanced class can use a Second Seal to return to
  level 1 of the **same** class. It keeps its stats and class skill, and keeps
  growing until it reaches the class's stat caps. No source names a limit on
  how many times you can do this.

> [!NOTE]
> Vander is the one real exception. He joins promoted with the lowest growth
> rates in the cast, and every source says he fades. Etie and Boucheron are
> the other early units most often called hard to keep.

### What grinding costs

Nothing, except real time. There is no calendar. After each battle the Somniel
moves to the next time of day (morning, afternoon, evening, night). That
changes only the fortune-teller's hours, which fish you can catch and where
your allies stand. The story never moves until you pick the next chapter.

The only side effect is skirmish scaling. Enemy level follows your
highest-level units. Every level your top team gains makes future skirmishes
harder.

### The catch-up recipe

Use these steps together on the unit you want to bring back. They are listed
roughly from most to least useful.

1. **Promote at level 10** with a Master Seal. Then reclass with a Second Seal
   only if you want to. Never use a Second Seal to go back into a **base**
   class, because the unit must climb 10 levels again.
2. **Stack EXP boosters on that unit.**
   - Byleth's **Mentorship**: ×1.2 for the user and adjacent allies. Byleth
     bond 3, or inherit it for 250 SP.
   - Edelgard's **Lineage** (Expansion Pass): ×1.2, 150 SP. Stacks with
     Mentorship for ×1.44.
   - Marth's **Mercurius** or Lucina's **Parthia**: ×2 while engaged. Both need
     bond level 10 with that Emblem.
3. **Give the unit a staff.** A healer earns EXP on every heal, whatever its
   stats. Micaiah's Engage Attack, **Great Sacrifice**, heals the whole army at
   once. Game8 puts it at about 120 EXP, more than one full level.
4. **Feed it kills in skirmishes.** Skirmish enemies are set to your **top**
   units' level, so a weak unit fights enemies above its own level. That gives
   it **more** EXP per kill. Bring 2 or 3 strong units. Let them weaken enemies,
   then let the weak unit take the kill. Pick a map with a narrow chokepoint.
   Kills give far more EXP than hits that do not kill.
5. **Use training battles.** These are skirmishes at Firene Castle, Brodia
   Castle or Solm Palace. Every survivor gets 30 EXP, and nobody dies, even in
   Classic.
6. **Use all 3 arena fights after every battle.** Put the weak unit in them
   with a Bond Ring equipped for SP. Players report the arena refreshes after
   any battle, skirmishes included.
7. **Rewind freely.** On Normal the rewind is unlimited, so a risky kill costs
   nothing.

> [!TIP]
> On Normal and Hard, players report that EXP is **kept after a game over**.
> If a skirmish goes badly after your weak unit gained a lot of EXP, you can
> let Alear fall, decline the rewind and retry. The unit keeps the EXP. SP,
> bond, items and drops are **not** kept. Three separate player threads
> describe this. It does not work on Maddening.

> [!NOTE]
> Skirmishes appear over real time (one every 3 hours) and after you clear
> battles (one per 3 battles on Normal, one per 5 on Hard). Game8 describes
> moving the Switch clock forward one day to spawn new ones. That fits the
> wiki's real-time rule, but this guide did not test it.

### Early units worth keeping

Players on Normal and Hard report that these early units work well all game
with investment: **Chloé, Louis, Yunaka, Alcryst, Citrinne, Lapis, Diamant and
Amber**. One player's Chapter 20 Hard team still ran Clanne, Alfred, Chloé,
Yunaka and Diamant. Another said a Céline Great Knight with Ike "carried the
game" on Hard / Classic.

Early units also have one real edge over late joiners. They can inherit
**Canter** from Sigurd before Chapter 10. Late joiners arrive after Sigurd's
ring is gone and cannot get it until Chapter 17.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> On Normal and Hard you do not need perfect play. A favourite at a slight
> disadvantage still clears every map. The recipe above makes catching up
> efficient, so you can keep the characters you care about without making the
> run deliberately weak.

## Combat basics

The rules that decide most fights. When a map feels impossible, check these
before you grind.

- **Doubling.** A unit attacks twice when its Speed is **5 or more** higher than
  the enemy's. Older Fire Emblem games used 4. The Speed on the stat screen
  already counts weapon weight.
- **Weapon triangle.** Sword beats Axe. Axe beats Lance. Lance beats Sword. Art
  (fists) beats Tome, Bow and Knife. The triangle changes **no** hit or damage
  numbers. It only causes Break.
- **Break.** If you start combat with triangle advantage and your hit lands, the
  target is **Broken**. A Broken unit cannot counterattack for that combat and
  the next one. Armored units, units on forts and some skills are immune.
- **Engage early and often.** The engage meter is full at the start of every
  map. It refills as you fight, or instantly on an Emblem Energy tile. Do not
  save engages for the boss.
- **Bait with bulky units.** Turn on the enemy range display. End each turn with
  fragile units out of range. Let a tough unit take the enemy phase.
- **Armor is weak to magic.** Keep armored units such as Louis away from mages.
- **Fliers are weak to bows.** Bring 1 or 2 bow users into the late game, where
  enemy fliers are common.
- **Rewind further back.** When a unit dies, the fatal mistake is often two or
  three moves earlier. Rewind to there.

> [!NOTE]
> Hard and Maddening give 10 rewinds per map. Normal gives unlimited rewinds. A
> remaining charge fires automatically on a Game Over.

## Common mistakes

The mistakes that players list most often in their "what I wish I knew" threads.
Check this list when the game starts to feel hard or unfair.

1. **Holding units back to level 20 before promotion.** In Engage there is no
   good reason to wait. Promote at level 10. See
   [Changing classes](#changing-classes).
2. **Spreading EXP across the whole army.** The game gives you more units than
   slots. Train about 12 at a time. Many strong units join already promoted
   after Chapter 11. If you want to keep a favourite, focus EXP on them rather
   than on everyone. See [Keeping early favourites](#keeping-early-favourites).
3. **Units with no ring.** A unit with no ring earns **zero SP**. Give every
   unit without an Emblem a Bond Ring. A Bond Ring earns half the SP of an
   Emblem ring.
4. **Saving engages for bosses.** Engage whenever it helps. The meter refills.
5. **Skipping bond conversations.** Bond EXP freezes at levels 5, 10 and 20
   until you watch the conversation in the Ring Chamber.
6. **Spending gold on outfits, limited items and high donation levels.** Gold is
   tight. Staffs are the purchase to protect.
7. **Doing Emblem paralogues the moment they open.** They do not scale. They
   are much easier a few chapters later.
8. **Forgetting to inherit before Chapter 10.** See the next section.
9. **Equipping an Emblem in the arena.** It earns no SP there. Use a Bond Ring.

### The Chapter 10 warning

The biggest trap in the game is a story event. At the end of Chapter 10 the
enemy **takes six of your rings**: Marth, Sigurd, Celica, Micaiah, Roy and Leif.
Chapter 10 leads straight into Chapter 11. You cannot visit the Somniel in
between.

> [!CAUTION]
> Before you start Chapter 10, do all of this:
>
> 1. Inherit every skill you want from those six Emblems. **Canter** from
>    Sigurd is the most important.
> 2. Get any weapon proficiency you need from their bond levels, for example
>    Axe, Bow or Knife from Leif.
> 3. Engrave weapons with their engravings, if you want them.
> 4. Pick your 10 best units. Up to 10 carry over into Chapter 11, and Chapter
>    11 has no preparation screen.
>
> The game warns you too. The rings come back between Chapters 17 and 22.

Inherited skills, spent SP, weapon proficiencies and engraved weapons all stay
on the unit when the ring goes. Only the ring's sync and engage effects leave.
See [What stays, what goes](#what-stays-what-goes).

> [!NOTE]
> No source states whether a unit's bond level with a lost Emblem is still
> there when the ring returns. Bond is stored per unit and Emblem, so it
> probably is. Treat that as likely, not confirmed. Either way, anything you
> **bought** with the bond before Chapter 10 is safe.

Expansion Pass bracelets are **not** taken.

## Team building

This answers question 4. On Normal and Hard the team is flexible. Players agree
that Engage lets you make almost any unit good with rings and inherited skills.
Tier lists are written for Maddening, the hardest difficulty, and you can
ignore them on Normal and Hard.

### Slots per chapter

How many units you can deploy, including Alear. Normal and Hard are the same.

| Chapters | Slots |
| --- | --- |
| 5–10 | 9 or 10 |
| 11–13 | 7 to 10 |
| 14–21 | 9 to 12 |
| 22–26 | 13 or 14 |

Train about 12 units. That covers every map up to the final four, where you
need 14.

### A balanced team

A good team covers these roles. It does not need to be exact.

- **2 or 3 healers** (staff users). Healing also gives them free EXP.
- **1 or 2 bow users**, for enemy fliers.
- **1 or 2 bulky units** to take hits on the enemy phase.
- **1 or 2 mages**, for enemy armor.
- **A few fast hitters** who double enemies.
- **Seadall**, the dancer, from Chapter 15. He lets another unit act again.

A Thief is optional. Every unit can open chests.

### What is not viable

No source says any composition fails on Normal or Hard. Hard-mode players say
their losses came from play habits, not from roster choice:

- They stood in purple miasma, which gives −20 Defense and Resistance.
- They did not inherit skills.
- They kept weak early units in the team too long.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **New recruits will outclass your early units. That is normal.** From
> Chapter 11, most new units arrive promoted or at level 15 to 19. One player
> put it this way: "You aren't doing something wrong if your 5th best unit looks
> like garbage compared to three new ones you were just handed." To keep an
> early favourite anyway, see
> [Keeping early favourites](#keeping-early-favourites).

## Changing classes

This answers question 2. Class changes in Engage work through two items and one
rule. The rule: **a unit can enter a class only if it has proficiency in every
weapon that class uses.**

### Promotion

- A **Master Seal** promotes a base class unit at **level 10 or higher**. It
  costs 2,500 gold.
- **Promote at level 10.** Players agree there is no practical gain in waiting
  until level 20, as in older games. Game8 tested it and got the same EXP from
  the same enemy in both classes.
- The wiki notes a hidden cost: each seal adds to a hidden "internal level" that
  lowers EXP slightly. Nobody has published the formula, so the size of the cost
  is unknown. Every source still recommends early promotion.
- An advanced class learns its class skill at level 5.
- Base and advanced classes cap at level 20. After level 20 in an advanced
  class, a Second Seal can reset the unit to level 1 of the same class. The unit
  keeps its stats and its class skill, and can keep growing.
- Special classes (Thief, Dancer, Fell Child) run from level 1 to 40 and never
  promote.

**Supply.** Master Seals are scarce early:

- Chapter 7 drops one. Anna's paralogue (after Chapter 6) has one in a chest.
  Chapters 10, 11, 16 and 17 also drop one.
- The shop sells 1 from Chapter 9. It adds 2 at each of Chapters 12, 14, 15
  and 17. From Chapter 18 the supply is unlimited.
- Spend the early seals on the units you use most. Chloé, Framme and Louis are
  commonly named first.

> [!NOTE]
> Game8 says the shop sells Master Seals from Chapter 13. The wiki's shop stock
> tables say Chapter 9. This guide follows the wiki.

### What the game intends

The class change screen shows every class in a list. Classes the unit can enter
are in **white** at the top. Classes it cannot enter are **grey**. The weapons
each class needs sit next to its name. The unit's weapons show in the bottom
left, and its **innate** weapon is the one shown in **blue**.

No source found a "recommended" marker on this screen. Use these rules instead:

1. A unit with a **personal class**, such as Alear, Alfred, Céline, Diamant,
   Alcryst, Ivy, Hortensia, Timerra or Fogado, has exactly one promotion. That
   is the default.
2. A unit in a **generic class** has two promotion options. With only its own
   weapons, usually only one option is white. That one is the default. The
   **Default promotion** column in [Characters to train](#characters-to-train)
   lists it.
3. The **innate** weapon (blue) hints at what suits the unit. In some advanced
   classes it also raises that weapon's maximum rank.

### Reclassing

A **Second Seal** (2,500 gold) changes a unit to another class. It has no level
requirement.

- **Promote first, then reclass.** A reclass sets the class level to 1. A base
  class unit that reclasses must climb to level 10 again before promotion.
- Class skills are **not** inherited. A unit loses its class skill when it
  leaves the class.
- Second Seals are scarce early: 3 in the shop from Chapter 9, more over time,
  and unlimited from Chapter 18.
- Reclassing is **optional** on Normal and Hard. Several players finished a
  first run with no Second Seals at all. Rings do most of the customising.

### Getting new weapons

A unit's own weapons are its only proficiencies until an Emblem adds more. Each
Emblem grants proficiency at a set bond level. The cheapest:

| Weapon | Emblem and bond level |
| --- | --- |
| Axe | Leif, bond 2 |
| Bow | Leif, bond 3. Lyn, bond 6 |
| Knife | Leif, bond 4. Micaiah, bond 6 |
| Staff | Micaiah, bond 3 |
| Tome | Celica, bond 6. Micaiah, bond 9 |
| Sword | Roy, Lucina or Corrin, bond 6 |
| Lance | Sigurd, bond 6 |
| Art | Byleth, bond 6 |

The proficiency stays after you remove the ring.

## Emblem rings

This answers question 5. The rings are Engage's main system. Each unit can
equip one ring. The ring does two things: **sync** while worn, and **engage**
when you trigger it.

### What stays, what goes

The single most useful fact about rings: **some benefits belong to the ring,
and some belong to the unit.** When you take a ring off, the ring's benefits
leave with it. The unit's benefits stay for the rest of the game.

| Benefit | Belongs to | When you remove the ring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sync stat bonuses and sync skills | The ring | Gone. The next wearer gets them, at **their own** bond level |
| Engage weapons, engage skills, Engage Attack | The ring, while engaged | Gone |
| **Bond level** with that Emblem | **The unit–Emblem pair** | **Kept.** The unit keeps its bond and can build on it later |
| **Inherited skills** | **The unit** | **Kept** |
| **Weapon proficiency** earned from bond | **The unit** | **Kept, permanently** |
| **SP** | **The unit** | **Kept** |

So a ring is a tool you lend out, and the skills, weapons and bond it leaves
behind are permanent upgrades. That is why passing rings around is good play,
not a waste.

### Passing rings around

You can move rings as often as you like. There is no cost and no penalty.

- **Where:** on the Somniel, on the world map (**+** button, then Emblem
  Rings), and on the pre-battle screen.
- **Not during a battle.** Once a map starts, the rings stay where they are.
- **One ring per unit, one unit per ring.** There is only one copy of each
  Emblem. If you give a ring to a new unit, the game takes it off the old one.
- **Bond is per unit.** If Marth moves from Alear to Kagetsu, Kagetsu starts at
  **his own** bond level with Marth (level 1 if they have never fought
  together). Alear keeps her bond level with Marth for later.
- **Any number of units can build bond with the same Emblem.** Each pair has its
  own level. One Emblem can teach skills to your whole army over time.

A common pattern: a unit wears an Emblem until bond level 5 or higher, inherits
the skill it wants, and then passes the ring to the next unit.

### Sync and engage

A ring has two states. Sync is always on. Engage is a button you press.

- **Sync** is the passive state while the ring is worn. The unit gets stat
  bonuses and the Emblem's sync skills. Both grow with that unit's bond level.
- **Engage** is a transformation that lasts **3 turns**. It starts on your turn
  and ends at the start of your fourth turn. Bond level 11 adds a fourth turn.
  While engaged the unit gets:
  - the Emblem's **engage weapons**, usable by any class. Weapons in Engage
    never break;
  - one or more **engage skills**;
  - one **Engage Attack**, usable **once per engage**. It never misses.
- **You can engage many times per map.** The only limit is the engage meter.
- **The engage meter** starts **full on every map**. It does not carry over
  between maps. It empties when the engage ends. It refills by about one step
  for each attack the unit makes or takes, and by using a staff on an ally. It
  fills at once if the unit ends its turn on an **Emblem Energy** tile.
- **Press Engage before the unit acts.** You can move first, then engage, then
  attack. There is no button to end an engage early.

> [!NOTE]
> The Fire Emblem Wiki counts the meter as 8 steps, one per attack. The Fandom
> wiki counts 6 combats. Players report that a double attack fills 2 steps,
> which supports the Fire Emblem Wiki. In practice, a unit that fights every
> turn can engage again every few turns.

### Bond levels

Bond level runs from 1 to 20 for each unit and Emblem pair. It is **capped at
10** until you clear that Emblem's paralogue. Expansion Pass bracelets have all
20 levels from the start.

- **Fighting** with the ring raises it. The unit must act. A unit that only
  waits or uses an item gains no bond.
- **Arena Emblem training** raises the bond between a unit and an Emblem you
  choose, for Bond Fragments. The unit does not need to wear the ring. It costs
  100 fragments per level up to level 5, then 300 per level to level 10, then
  500 per level to level 20.
- **Ring polishing** in the Ring Chamber gives a small bond gain between the
  ring's Emblem and the unit you pick. It is available again after each battle.
- **Bond conversations** at levels 5, 10 and 20 block further bond EXP until you
  watch them in the Ring Chamber.

What bond levels unlock, for most Emblems:

| Bond level | Unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | First engage weapon |
| 5 | Skill inheritance starts |
| Varies (2 to 9) | Weapon proficiencies. See [Getting new weapons](#getting-new-weapons) |
| 10 | Second engage weapon. Needs a watched conversation |
| 11 | Engage lasts 4 turns |
| 15 | Third engage weapon |
| 20 | Engage meter is one step shorter |

  and Axe Fighter lines).
- Players call **Micaiah, Byleth and Lucina** the three most useful support
  Emblems. Give them to units that are not your main fighters.

## Skill inheritance

Inheritance is where the rings pay off permanently. A unit spends its own SP
to learn an Emblem's skill. The skill stays with the unit for the rest of the
game, whichever ring it wears.

### How it works

1. **The unit earns SP.** It gets 1 SP per 1 EXP with an Emblem ring, and 1 SP
   per 2 EXP with a Bond Ring. It gets **none with no ring**. Each unit has its
   own SP pool. SP keeps building at max level.
2. **The unit builds bond** with an Emblem to level 5 or higher. Each skill has
   its own bond level on top of that.
3. **You buy the skill** in the Ring Chamber, or at the Arena. The unit does
   **not** need to wear that ring at the time. It needs only the bond level and
   the SP.
4. **You equip it.** A unit can learn any number of skills, but can equip only
   **2 inherited skills** at a time. Change them in Manage Skills on the
   Somniel or on the pre-battle screen. Changing them is free.

Rules worth knowing:

- **SP cannot be refunded.** Choose with some care, but a wrong pick on Normal
  or Hard costs little.
- **Every unit can learn the same skill.** There is no limit. Ten units can all
  inherit Canter, if each pays the SP.
- **Higher tiers cost less** if you already bought the lower tier. You pay only
  the difference.
- **Inherit from Emblems the unit is not wearing.** A skill often does not stack
  with the same Emblem's sync skill while that ring is worn.
- **Some skills cannot be inherited:** engage skills, Engage Attacks, and a few
  sync skills such as Dragon Vein and Dual Strike.
- Inherited skills and SP spent **survive the Chapter 10 ring loss**.

> [!NOTE]
> An older Game8 page says units have three skill slots. That came from
> pre-release footage. Every current source, including Game8's own inheritance
> page, says two.

## Bond rings and engraving

Two smaller ring systems. Neither is essential on Normal or Hard, but Bond Rings
matter for one reason: SP.

### Bond rings

Bond Rings are small stat rings for units without an Emblem. They have no bond
level and cannot engage.

- They cost **100 Bond Fragments** each, or **1,000 for ten**, in the Ring
  Chamber.
- Ranks are C (60%), B (30%), A (7%) and S (3%). Only S rank carries a skill.
- You can merge copies: 2 C into 1 B, 3 B into 1 A, 4 A into 1 S.
- **Do one ten-pull early** so every unit without an Emblem earns SP.

> [!TIP]
> Players describe a save-and-reload trick to pick the ring you want. Most say
> it is not worth the time on Normal or Hard. The stats are small.

### Engraving

Engraving puts an Emblem's mark on a weapon at the smithy, after Chapter 5. It
costs Bond Fragments (Game8 says 100 to 1,000) and gives small permanent stat
changes. Each engraving can sit on one weapon at a time.

- Game8 recommends Marth (Beginnings) or Sigurd (Holy) as safe choices.
- Each Emblem's engraving sits on **one weapon at a time**. Engraving a second
  weapon removes it from the first.
- An engraving already on a weapon **stays** when the ring is lost at Chapter
  10. But you cannot put a lost Emblem's engraving on a new weapon until the
  ring returns. Engrave before Chapter 10 if you want them.

## Region donations

This answers question 6. You donate gold to a country at the café notice board.
Each donation raises that country's level, from 1 to 5. The short answer:
**level 2 everywhere is worth it, Brodia level 3 is worth it, and levels 4 and 5
are not.** Nothing essential is locked behind donations.

### Costs

| To reach | Cost | Running total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 2 | 5,000 | 5,000 |
| Level 3 | 10,000 | 15,000 |
| Level 4 | 25,000 | 40,000 |
| Level 5 | 50,000 | 90,000 |

Maxing all four countries costs 360,000 gold.

### What you get

Each level gives a one-time reward and a lasting bonus.

- **Level 2** gives a bundle and 500 fragments. Firene gives food. Brodia gives
  10 iron ingots. Elusia gives a Physic staff. Solm gives meat, fish and other
  food. It also unlocks that country's animals for the farm.
- **Level 3** gives a weapon, an outfit and 1,000 fragments.
- **Levels 4 and 5** give weapons you can mostly buy or find, plus more
  fragments.
- **Lasting bonuses:** more ingots from exploration, more gold from skirmishes,
  and 1,000 gold per level from training battles in that country.

**Ingot multiplier per level** (levels 1 to 5):

| Country | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Brodia | ×1, ×3, ×5, ×7, ×10 |
| Firene, Elusia, Solm | ×1, ×2, ×2, ×3, ×3 |

1. Firene to level 2 after Chapter 4.
2. Brodia to level 3 after Chapter 7.
3. Solm to level 2 after Chapter 12.

## Supports

Supports are the friendship ranks between two units: C, B and A. Only Alear can
reach S rank, with the Pact Ring. The bonuses are small, so do not plan around
them.

- Units gain support points by fighting next to each other, healing each other,
  sparring in the arena, eating together and using the support spots.
- A unit gets small Hit, Avoid and Crit bonuses when it fights **next to** a
  support partner. The bonus stacks for up to 4 partners.
- You must watch each support conversation before points build toward the next
  rank.

| Difficulty | What changes | Rewinds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Normal** | Most enemies get −20 Hit and −20 Avoid. Promoted enemies lose their class skills | Unlimited |
| **Hard** | Stronger enemies, more of them. Some bosses get revival stones. Less EXP from kills | 10 per map |
| **Maddening** | Stronger and smarter enemies. Less EXP again. Fixed growth rates. Skirmishes do not appear over time | 10 per map |

You can **lower** the difficulty at any time at the bed in your room on the
Somniel. You can never raise it.

**Which to pick:**

- **Normal** if you want the story first, or if you quit before because the game
  felt like too much. Players tell new players to learn on Normal and play
  harder next time.
- **Hard** if you have played strategy games before and want the maps to "have
  some teeth". A first-ever player on Hard / Classic lost three units by
  Chapter 5 and called it "dang impossible".
- **Maddening** is not for a first run. Nearly all build and tier advice online
  is written for it.

### Casual or Classic

- **Casual:** a fallen unit returns after the battle.
- **Classic:** a fallen unit is gone for good.
- You **cannot switch** between them after you start. Guide sites say so and no
  source says otherwise.
- In both modes, losing Alear ends the game.

> [!TIP]
> Casual removes the fear of a bad move. With rewinds on top, it makes a
> "bricked" save almost impossible. Choose Classic only if permanent loss is what
> makes the game exciting for you.

## Expansion Pass

Optional paid content. Skip this section if you do not own it.

- After Chapter 6, a Somniel event gives the **Edelgard, Dimitri and Claude**
  bracelet and opens the **Divine Paralogues**. These give **Tiki, Hector,
  Soren, Camilla, Chrom and Robin, and Veronica**. They do scale, and you can
  replay them.
- DLC bracelets work like rings and are **not** taken at Chapter 10.
- The Expansion Pass gives 5,000 Bond Fragments and 30,000 gold after Chapter 5.
- The **Fell Xenologue** is a separate story with its own difficulty setting.
  Players say to play it on Normal. Game8 suggests waiting until about Chapter 15
  or 16. It adds Nel, Rafal, Zelestia, Gregory and Madeline.
- Useful DLC skills: **Lineage** from Edelgard (+20% EXP, 150 SP) and
  **Starsphere** from Tiki (better growth on level-up).
