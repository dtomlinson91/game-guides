# Three Houses beginner guide

A reference for a first playthrough of *Fire Emblem: Three Houses* on Nintendo
Switch, at **Hard / Classic**, with the **Expansion Pass** installed. It answers
the four questions a new player asks most: when to grind, how class changes
work, how to spend free time, and how to build a team.

Read the next section first. Then use the rest as a lookup while you play.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> You cannot brick this save. Character level does not reset when you change
> class, you keep every class you pass an exam for, and bad level-up luck across
> a whole game costs about four or five stat points. The only true one-way
> decisions are your house, the single Dancer, and the recruiting window that
> closes at the end of Part 1. Everything else you can correct later.

> [!TIP]
> You can lower the difficulty at any time from the system menu. You can never
> raise it. Starting on Hard therefore costs you nothing, because Normal stays
> available if Hard stops being fun.

## The weekly loop

Each in-game week runs the same way. This is the routine you repeat about 90
times, so learn it once and the game stops feeling large.

A month holds four or five weeks. The last week is the story mission. Every
other week gives you a lesson on Monday and a free day on Sunday.

Do these steps in order each week.

1. **Set goals.** Give each unit two skills to study, or one skill to
   specialise in. One skill pays 1.5 times the experience of that skill inside
   a two-skill pair.
2. **Instruct.** Teach as many units as your lecture points allow. Your
   professor level sets that limit. See
   [The Sunday choice](#the-sunday-choice).
3. **Answer the student question.** A student sometimes asks you something
   after instruction. A correct answer is the largest single source of
   professor experience in the game.
4. **Handle goal-change requests.** A student may ask to change their goals.
   Accepting or declining changes nothing mechanically. Accept when the goal
   matches the class you want for them.
5. **Assign a group task.** Pick two units. This trains a movement skill and
   pays gold and Smithing Stones.
6. **Spend Sunday.** Choose Explore, Fight, Seminar or Rest. See
   [The Sunday choice](#the-sunday-choice).

### Instruction numbers

Each unit holds up to 100 motivation, in steps of 25. One instruction costs 25
motivation. A unit at full motivation therefore takes four instructions in one
week.

Each instruction rolls a result. The result multiplies the skill experience.

| Result | Weakness | Neutral | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bad (×0.5) | +1 | +2 | +4 |
| Good (×1) | +2 | +4 | +6 |
| Great (×1.5) | +3 | +6 | +9 |
| Perfect (×2) | +4 | +8 | +12 |

Three rules follow from that table.

- A unit can only get "Bad" if it started the week at 50 motivation or less.
  Keep motivation high and you delete the worst outcome.
- The first "Perfect" of the week returns 25 motivation. That unit can then take
  a fifth instruction.
- After a "Bad" result you choose to critique or console. The right choice for
  that character returns 25 motivation.

### Goal and task numbers

Goals pay out at the end of the week. **These values are lower on Hard than on
Normal.**

| Difficulty | Weakness | Neutral | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | +24 | +28 | +32 |
| **Hard** | **+20** | **+24** | **+28** |
| Maddening | +16 | +20 | +24 |

Group tasks train Riding (Stable Duty), Heavy Armour (Weeding) or Flying (Sky
Watch) for two units.

| Result | Reward | Weakness | Neutral | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Good | 3 Smithing Stones, 500 gold | +4 | +8 | +12 |
| Perfect | 5 Smithing Stones, 1,000 gold | +8 | +12 | +16 |

A "Perfect" result is more likely when both units have a strength in the skill
and a high support rank with each other.

### Skill rank costs

Skill experience buys rank. The cost rises steeply, so plan the two skills a
unit needs and ignore the rest.

| Rank | Experience to the next rank |
| --- | --- |
| E | 40 |
| E+ | 60 |
| D | 80 |
| D+ | 120 |
| C | 160 |
| C+ | 220 |
| B | 280 |
| B+ | 360 |

> [!NOTE]
> Fighting also trains skills. Each combat gives +1 to +3 in one weapon, one
> movement skill and Authority, depending on strength or weakness. That is small
> per hit, but it adds up across a map and it costs nothing.

## The Sunday choice

Every free day offers four options. You pick one. This is the decision that
shapes your month, so it deserves a table of its own.

| Option | What it spends | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Explore** | Activity points | Professor experience, supports, motivation, skills, items, recruiting |
| **Fight** | Battle points | Level experience, class mastery, gold, ores |
| **Seminar** | Nothing | Skill experience for a group of units at once |
| **Rest** | Nothing | A small motivation refill for every student, plus 5 durability on the Sword of the Creator |

Your professor level sets both point pools. This is why professor level is the
most valuable thing you can build early.

| Professor level | Activity points | Lecture points | Battle points | Adjutants | Monthly gold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E | 1 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 1,000 |
| E+ | 2 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 2,000 |
| D | 3 | 4 | 1 | 0 | 3,000 |
| D+ | 4 | 4 | 1 | 0 | 4,000 |
| **C** | 5 | 5 | 1 | **1** | 5,000 |
| C+ | 6 | 5 | **2** | 1 | 5,000 |
| B | 7 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 5,000 |
| B+ | 8 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 5,000 |
| A | 9 | 7 | 2 | 3 | 5,000 |
| A+ | 10 | 7 | 3 | 3 | 5,000 |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Professor level **C** is a hard gate. Below C you cannot take Master class
> exams, and you cannot even see what those classes require. Reach C before you
> plan any Master class.

A reasonable default split is **two Explore days and two Fight days per month**.
Lean towards Explore in the first few months, because professor level compounds
into everything else. Use Seminar when several units are behind on one skill.
Use Rest only when class motivation has collapsed.

## Exploration order

Exploring is where the game hides most of its value, and where most new players
waste points. The key fact is that **most exploration activities are free**.

### Free activities

Do all of these every single Explore day, before you spend a point on anything.

| Activity | Gives |
| --- | --- |
| Fishing | 10 to 40 professor experience per fish, 100 for a Goddess Messenger. Needs bait, not points |
| Greenhouse | Stat-boosting items, gifts and seeds. 100 professor experience per grade of the lowest seed used. Once per free day |
| Giving gifts | Support with Byleth. Doubled for a liked gift, zero for a disliked one |
| Returning lost items | Support **and** motivation. Available from Chapter 3 |
| Quests | Items, gold, renown, and new facilities |
| Counselor advice | Support with the student who asked |
| Recruiting | See [Recruiting students](#recruiting-students) |
| Watching supports | Support ranks, which feed adjutants and gambits |

> [!TIP]
> The Owl Feather is liked by every character in the game. Buy them whenever a
> merchant stocks them and use them on anyone whose gift list you do not know.

### Paid activities

Seven activities cost one activity point each. Spend your points in roughly
this order.

1. **Faculty Training.** 20 skill experience for Byleth, plus 10 more for a
   strength. A "Great" result adds 50%. Byleth's budding talent needs six
   sessions in Faith. Each instructor teaches only their own skills, and several
   are absent in specific chapters.
2. **Share a Meal.** Raises support between two characters and Byleth, raises
   their motivation, and pays 50 to 200 professor experience depending on
   whether they like the dish. Today's Special costs no ingredients, once per
   Sunday.
3. **Choir Practice.** Raises Faith for both students and Byleth, and Authority
   for Byleth. The cheapest Authority Byleth will ever get.
4. **Tea Party.** Support with one unit, and up to +2 Charm for both if you pick
   good topics. Charm drives gambit accuracy, so this is not cosmetic.
5. **Sauna** (Expansion Pass). Support, plus a skill-experience boost for both
   units for the rest of the month. One visit per unit per month.
6. **Cooking Together.** Support with one unit and 75 to 150 professor
   experience.
7. **Tournament.** Gold and weapons. 100 professor experience for a loss, 300
   for a win.

> [!NOTE]
> The Fire Emblem Wiki lists five activities as costing activity points and
> leaves Tea Party and Sauna off that list. Its own Tea Party and Sauna sections
> both state a cost of one point. I have followed the specific sections.

### Experience thresholds

Professor level rises on a cumulative total. The early ranks come fast and the
late ones do not, so the first few months are where the compounding happens.

| Level | Total experience |
| --- | --- |
| E+ | 100 |
| D | 1,500 |
| D+ | 3,600 |
| C | 6,400 |
| C+ | 10,900 |
| B | 16,300 |
| B+ | 24,000 |
| A | 32,800 |
| A+ | 44,500 |

The lecture question dwarfs everything else. At professor level C and above it
pays 1,000 experience, or 1,200 if the student likes the answer. Compare that to
30 for a good fish.

### Renown and statues

Renown comes from battles and quests. Spend it at the four saint statues in the
cathedral, from Chapter 5. Costs are cumulative, so 10,000 renown clears one
statue completely. Rewards carry into New Game+.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | Lance +1 | Axe +1 | Sword +1 | Bow +1 |
| 500 | Faith +1 | Authority +1 | Reason +1 | Brawling +1 |
| 1,000 | Experience +5% | Experience +5% | Experience +5% | Experience +5% |
| 2,000 | Class Mastery +1 | Flying +2 | Riding +2 | Heavy Armour +2 |
| 3,000 | Divine Pulse +1 | Divine Pulse +1 | Divine Pulse +1 | Divine Pulse +1 |
| 4,000 | Lance +2 | Axe +2 | Sword +2 | Bow +2 |
| 5,000 | Faith +2 | Authority +2 | Reason +2 | Brawling +2 |
| 7,500 | Experience +10% | Experience +10% | Experience +10% | Experience +10% |

Pulse charges next.

> The weapon and skill rewards such as "Axe +1" add experience when you
> **instruct** in that skill. They do not change combat.

## When to grind

This is the question that worries most new Hard players, and the answer is
short: **on Hard you cannot over-grind, and you rarely need to.**

### Why grinding is capped

On Normal, two auxiliary battle options per free day cost nothing, so grinding
is unlimited. On Hard and Maddening, **every auxiliary battle and every
paralogue consumes a battle point**. You hold 1 battle point until professor
level C+, then 2, then 3 at A+.

With about four free days a month, a professor at C+ can fight at most eight
times, and only by spending every Sunday on battle and never exploring. That
trade is the real cost of grinding.

> [!NOTE]
> The Fire Emblem Wiki's page on auxiliary battles says they cost "an activity
> point" on Hard. The in-game pools are separate, and the same wiki's professor
> level page says battle points govern how many battles you may fight. I have
> used battle points throughout. The practical effect is identical: on Hard,
> nothing about fighting is free.

### Reasons to grind

Four situations justify spending a Sunday on a battle rather than on the
monastery.

1. **A unit you need is more than two levels behind the squad.** Deploy them in
   an auxiliary battle and let them take the kills.
2. **You want a class mastery.** Mastery is counted in combats, not in
   experience, so it only comes from fighting. See the counts in
   [Changing class](#changing-class).
3. **You are short of gold or crafting materials.** Auxiliary battles pay gold
   and monastery items, and human bosses drop a bullion.
4. **A paralogue is available.** Always take it. A paralogue costs the same
   battle point as an auxiliary battle, is harder, and pays far better. Some
   paralogues also decide whether a character returns after the timeskip.

### Reasons not to

Two situations make a battle the worse choice.

- Everything is clearing comfortably. Experienced Hard players report finishing
  the route with almost no grinding at all.
- You have not yet reached professor level C. An Explore day is worth more than
  a battle at that stage, because it raises every future month's point budget.

### Cheaper catch-up routes

Three mechanics raise a lagging unit without costing a battle point.

- **Adjutants.** An adjutant gains 100% of skill experience, 100% of class
  mastery experience and 50% of level experience, without occupying a
  deployment slot. This is the cheapest catch-up in the game.
- **Seminars.** Free skill experience for a group.
- **The Knowledge Gem.** Doubles skill and class mastery experience for the
  holder. The Experience Gem does the same for level experience.

## Changing class

Three Houses has no fixed promotion lines. Any unit can enter any class, if they
pass the exam. That freedom is why the system feels unclear, so here is the
shape of it.

### How an exam works

Take exams from the Certification option on the calendar screen, at a weekend or
before a mission.

| Tier | From level | Item needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | 5 | Beginner Seal |
| Intermediate | 10 | Intermediate Seal (Dark Seal for Dark Mage) |
| Advanced | 20 | Advanced Seal |
| Special (DLC) | 20 | Abyssian Exam Pass |
| Master | 30 | Master Seal, **and professor level C or higher** |

The exam screen shows a success rate and the suggested skill levels. The closer
your skills are to the suggestion, the higher the rate. Below 30% the game
refuses the attempt. The seal is consumed whether you pass or fail.

Abyssian Exam Passes come from the Pagan Altar in Abyss for 750 renown each,
without limit, from Chapter 1 onward. Yuri, Balthus, Constance and Hapi each
arrive carrying one.

### Rules that matter

Five rules explain most of the confusion around this system.

1. **Level does not reset.** Unlike older Fire Emblem games, promotion costs you
   nothing. Certify as soon as you pass, unless you are deliberately staying to
   finish a mastery.
2. **A pass is permanent.** Once you pass an exam you never retake it. You can
   switch between any passed class from the Inventory menu before a battle.
3. **Class changing raises low stats.** Any stat below the new class's base is
   pulled up to that base. The class also grants stat modifiers while you stay
   in it.
4. **Almost every class can use almost every weapon.** Two exceptions only.
   Gauntlets cannot be used on a horse or a flier. Reason and Faith magic only
   work in a magic class.
5. **Mastery is counted in combats.** One combat gives one mastery point, even
   if you did not initiate it.

| Class tier | Combats to master |
| --- | --- |
| Noble, Commoner | 20 |
| Beginner | 60 |
| Intermediate | 100 |
| Advanced and Special | 150 |
| Master | 200 |

The Cethleann statue reward at 2,000 renown adds +1 per combat. The Knowledge
Gem or the Mastermind ability doubles it. Both together triple it, not
quadruple it.

Mastery abilities can be equipped in any class. Combat arts from Beginner and
Intermediate classes carry over too. Combat arts from Advanced classes and above
are locked to that class.

### Masteries worth planning

Most masteries are minor. These are the ones experienced players build towards,
because the ability they grant outlives the class you learned it in.

| Class | Tier | Mastery reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brigand | Intermediate | Death Blow (+6 Strength when you initiate) |
| Cavalier | Intermediate | Desperation |
| Mercenary | Intermediate | Vantage |
| Archer | Intermediate | Hit +20 |
| Mage | Intermediate | Fiendish Blow (+6 Magic when you initiate) |
| Pegasus Knight | Intermediate | Darting Blow (+6 Attack Speed when you initiate) |
| Thief | Intermediate | Steal |
| Grappler | Advanced | Tomebreaker, Fierce Iron Fist |
| Sniper | Advanced | Hunter's Volley |
| War Master | Master | Quick Riposte, War Master's Strike |

> [!CAUTION]
> Written guides tell every physical unit to master Brigand for Death Blow, and
> every unit to pick up Hit +20. **That advice is written for Maddening.** A
> long-time Hard player states plainly that both are close to redundant on Hard,
> because you fight on the enemy phase often — where Death Blow does nothing —
> and your hit rates are already fine. Treat Death Blow as optional on Hard, not
> as a requirement. I have followed the Hard-specific account, because the
> guide-site claim is written for a different difficulty.

## Team shape

You do not need an optimal team on Hard. You need a balanced one. Nothing in
this game is strictly unviable at this difficulty, so read this as a shape to
aim at rather than a rule.

### How many units

Most maps let you field about ten to twelve units, and adjutants add one to
three more by professor level. Train **twelve to fifteen** units seriously and
bench the rest.

> [!NOTE]
> I could not find a published per-chapter table of deployment slots. The "about
> ten to twelve" figure comes from experienced players rather than from game
> data. Check the preparations screen for the real number each map.

Training everybody is the most common new-player mistake. Experience is finite
on Hard, so twenty half-built units is worse than thirteen finished ones.

### Roles to cover

A team that covers these five roles will clear every map on Hard. Counts assume
about twelve deployment slots.

| Role | How many | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enemy-phase unit | 2 | Stands in the open and kills what attacks it. Wants high Protection or high Avoid, plus Vantage or Wrath |
| Player-phase damage | 6 to 8 | Kills one target on your turn. Wants combat arts and "blow" abilities |
| Healer | 2 | At least one with Physic for range. A Bishop doubles white magic uses |
| Dancer | 1 | Gives one ally an extra turn. Only one per playthrough |
| Flier | 1 to 2 | Ignores terrain and carries the map |

Keep both physical and magical damage. Armoured enemies have high Protection and
low Resilience, so a pure physical team stalls against them.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Take the Dancer seriously. Giving your best unit a second action every turn is
> usually worth more than anything the Dancer could do on their own. New players
> routinely skip this and then wonder why maps feel slow.

### Doubling

A unit attacks twice when its Attack Speed is **4 or more** above the target's.

```
Attack Speed = Speed − max(0, weapon weight − Strength / 5)
```

Two consequences. A heavy weapon on a low-Strength unit removes doubling. And
unequipping a weapon entirely can stop an enemy doubling *you*, which sometimes
saves a unit.

### Real constraints

These are the only hard limits on team building.

- Fliers may equip **flying battalions only**.
- Gauntlets do not work on a mount, horse or flier.
- Reason and Faith magic work only in magic classes.
- Several classes are gender-locked. Male only: Brawler, Dark Mage, Hero,
  Grappler, Dark Bishop, War Master. Female only: Pegasus Knight, Falcon Knight,
  Gremory, Dark Flier, Valkyrie.

## Battalions and gambits

Battalions are the largest source of combat power that a new player ignores.
They are gated behind the Authority skill, which is easy to miss because no
class requires much of it.

### Battalions

A battalion is a squad attached to one unit. It grants flat stats and it takes
damage on that unit's behalf.

- A unit may equip a battalion at or below its Authority rank.
- Movement type restricts choice. Infantry, armoured and cavalry units may take
  any battalion. Fliers take flying battalions only.
- A battalion adds flat attack, hit, critical, avoid, protection, resilience and
  charm. It levels from 1 to 5 and the bonuses grow.
- A battalion has endurance. It absorbs **half** the damage its unit takes. At 0
  endurance the unit loses both the stat bonus and the gambit.
- Endurance does **not** refill after a battle. Repair it at the battalion
  guild. A battalion at 0 is never lost permanently.

> [!TIP]
> Train Authority on everybody, and aim for about B by the timeskip. A strong
> battalion is worth roughly +10 damage according to experienced players, which
> is more than many level-ups give. Sort the battalion list by attack with the X
> button and take the best one each unit can hold.

### Gambits

A gambit is the battalion's special attack. Each has limited uses per battle,
and the uses refill at the end of the map.

- A gambit can never be counterattacked, can never critical, and can never
  double.
- Accuracy runs on **Charm**, not Dexterity. The gap between your Charm and the
  target's drives the hit rate, capped at ±30.
- A hit applies *rattled*: the target's movement drops to zero, it loses its
  battalion bonus, and it cannot use a gambit.
- A gambit only kills its primary target. Others in the area survive at 1 HP.
- **Gambit Boost**: allies who could also attack the target add attack and hit,
  scaled by their support rank with the user. Up to three allies plus an
  adjutant count.

Players repeatedly name the same utility gambits as the ones that solve maps:
Stride, Impregnable Wall, Retribution, Blessing and Dance of the Goddess. The
offensive gambits matter less than the movement and defence ones.

## Adjutants and supports

Adjutants unlock at professor level C. They are a second unit attached to a
deployed unit, and they are the main reason support ranks matter mechanically.

- Slots by professor level: 1 at C, 2 at B, 3 at A.
- An adjutant gains 50% of the host's level experience, 100% of skill experience
  (except Authority) and 100% of class mastery experience.
- A flying host may only take a flying adjutant.
- If the host falls, the adjutant leaves the field unharmed.
- An adjutant gives no stat bonus to the host.

The adjutant's own class decides which of three behaviours it gives.

| Behaviour | Classes | Effect | None / C / B / A / S |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Follow-Up | Most classes | Extra attack after combat, player phase only | 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50% |
| Guard | Brawler, Armoured Knight, Grappler, Fortress Knight, Great Knight, War Monk, War Cleric | Reduces damage from enemy follow-up attacks, and leaves the host on 1 HP instead of dying | 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50% |
| Heal | Monk, Priest, Bishop, Holy Knight | Heals the host at turn start below 50% HP | 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 heals |

> [!WARNING]
> Because of a bug, the C+, B+ and A+ support ranks are treated as **no
> support** for Adjutant Follow-Up, giving only 10%. The bug was fixed in
> version 1.1.0 and reintroduced in 1.2.0. Plan around full ranks.

## Recruiting students

From Chapter 2 you may recruit students from the other two houses, plus faculty.
This is how you fix any gap in your roster.

### How the check works

A student needs **one stat value and one skill rank** from Byleth. A faculty
member needs only a character level. Failing, or declining their offer, locks
the option until the next weekend.

Support rank with Byleth lowers both requirements.

| Support | Skill rank D → | C → | B → | B+ → |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None | D | C | B | B+ |
| C | D | C | C+ | B |
| C+ | D | D+ | C | C+ |
| B | E+ | D | D+ | D+ |
| B+ | E+ | E+ | D | D+ |
| A | Any | Any | Any | Any |

| Support | Stat 10 → | 15 → | 20 → | 25 → | 30 → |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 |
| C | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 |
| C+ | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 | 18 |
| B | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
| B+ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| A | Any | Any | Any | Any | Any |

> [!TIP]
> **The shortcut that skips all of this.** At B support or higher, a student may
> walk up on any free weekday and ask to join your house. That offer ignores the
> stat and skill requirements completely. Build supports through meals, gifts,
> lost items and tea, and most of the roster recruits itself. The offer is
> random, so you may get none in a week or several.

> [!WARNING]
> The shortcut does not work for **Caspar or Ferdinand**. You cannot reach B
> support with either during Part 1.

> [!NOTE]
> Stat boosts from a class do **not** count towards the requirement, and neither
> do stat-boosting abilities. If a check fails when you think it should pass,
> reclass to Commoner or Noble and try again.

### Who you cannot recruit

Edelgard, Hubert, Dimitri, Dedue and Claude are never recruitable. Hilda cannot
be recruited on the Black Eagles route until Chapter 12, and only if you side
with the church.

**Recruiting closes at the end of Part 1.** Anna, from the Expansion Pass, is
the only exception.

## Common mistakes

If something feels wrong mid-run, check this list before anything else. These
are the failures that experienced players name most often.

1. **You split your units up.** The single most reported cause of a lost unit is
   sending one character too far ahead. Move as a block. Let the enemy come.
2. **You ignored battalions and Authority.** No class demands Authority, so it
   quietly never gets trained, and then your units are missing +10 damage.
3. **You are not using the Dancer.**
4. **Motivation has collapsed.** A class below 50 motivation starts getting
   "Bad" instruction results at half value. Spend a Rest day, or serve more
   meals.
5. **You spread skill training too thin.** Pick the two skills a unit's target
   class needs. Everything else is waste.
6. **Weapons and battalions are not repaired.** Enemies on Hard take more hits
   to kill, so durability runs out faster than on Normal.
7. **You are training too many units.** Cut to twelve to fifteen.
8. **You are following Maddening advice.** Maddening is a different game. Its
   guides assume you never fight on the enemy phase and never double. Neither
   assumption holds on Hard.

## Windows you can miss

A short list of things that are genuinely one-way. Nothing here ruins a run, but
each is easier to catch than to regret.

- **Recruiting closes at the end of Part 1.** Decide who you want before
  Chapter 12.
- **One Dancer per playthrough.** The White Heron Cup picks them. Enter a unit
  with high Charm. Players report that speaking to your chosen entrant again
  after selecting them grants a Charm bonus.
- **Byleth's C supports lock after a certain chapter.** The game tells you after
  the fact when a support can no longer advance.
- **Paralogues can decide a character's fate.** Completing the right paralogue
  brings Ashe or Lorenz back after the timeskip instead of losing them.
- **The Black Eagles route split needs setup.** You need C+ support with
  Edelgard and a specific conversation with her during the month before
  Chapter 11. Without it you get the default route.
- **Lost items spawn monthly from Chapter 3.** Pick them up as you pass. You can
  return them at any later point.
- **The Sacred Items Set** sits on Byleth's bed if you own the Expansion Pass.
  It gives +7 HP, +3 Strength, +3 Speed and +2 Movement, once per playthrough,
  as a set.

### Movement classes worth noting

Cavalry classes grant **Canto**, which lets a unit move again after acting.
Flying classes grant Canto as well and ignore terrain. Those two properties are
why mounted and flying classes dominate most tier lists, far more than raw
stats do.

## Character reference

Strengths raise skill experience. Weaknesses lower it. A budding talent starts
neutral or weak and becomes a strength once unlocked, and grants an ability or
combat art.

Unlock a budding talent with **twelve instructions** in that skill. The first
star appears at four. Byleth is the exception: six faculty training sessions,
one star every two.

unsure where to take someone.

## Choosing a house

You read this once. The choice is permanent within a playthrough, and it decides
which eight students you get free, which route you play, and which characters
you cannot have.

| House | Leader | Roster character | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Black Eagles | Edelgard | Magic-heavy | The only house with a route split. Fewer paralogues and fewer recruitable characters than the other two |
| Blue Lions | Dimitri | Physical, lance and cavalry | Most commonly recommended first route. Strong character-driven story |
| Golden Deer | Claude | Mixed, bow-leaning | Loses nobody to the plot. Explains the most about the world |

For a first Hard run I would take **Blue Lions or Golden Deer**. Black Eagles has
a magic-heavy roster, which makes the early chapters harder, and its route split
adds a decision you cannot evaluate on a blind playthrough.

> [!NOTE]
> Choosing a house does not lock you out of most characters. Every student
> except Edelgard, Hubert, Dimitri, Dedue and Claude can be recruited into any
> house, and the B-support shortcut makes that easy. Pick the house whose leader
> and story you want, not the one with the best statistics.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> If you pick Black Eagles and want the route split, reach C+ support with
> Edelgard and talk to her during the month before Chapter 11. Miss that and you
> get the default route with no warning.

Five points where the sources conflict, and which one this guide follows.

| Subject | Disagreement | What this guide follows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Death Blow and Hit +20 | Guide sites call them near-mandatory. A long-time Hard player calls both close to redundant on Hard | The Hard-specific account. The guide-site claim is written for Maddening |
| Weekly goal experience | Serenes Forest lists 28 neutral and 32 strength | Those are Normal values. Hard is 24 and 28 |
| Auxiliary battle cost | Fire Emblem Wiki says "activity point". Its own professor level page says battle point | Battle points. The effect is the same on Hard — nothing is free |
| Dark Flier abilities | Serenes Forest repeats the War Monk row | Fire Emblem Wiki: Canto, Black Tomefaire, Transmute |
| Maddening advice | One player says it transfers to Hard, another says it does not | It largely does not. Hard supports enemy-phase play and reliable doubling, which Maddening does not |

### Not verified

Three claims in this guide rest on player reports alone. Treat them as likely
rather than confirmed.

- **Deployment slots per chapter.** No published data table found. The figure of

- **The level at which the Ashen Wolves join.** Reported by a player as low, in
  a basic class. Not confirmed against game data.
- **The White Heron Cup Charm bonus** for speaking to your entrant again. Player
  report only.
